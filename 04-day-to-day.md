# Day-to-Day Ownership

Clearing the delivery and first-24-hours checks doesn't mean quality-control
issues are behind you. A few things worth keeping an eye on over time:

- [ ] **Door rubber seals**, particularly at the door bottoms — watch for
      loosening or gaps developing over time.
- [ ] **Tail light condensation** — wide temperature swings (e.g. parking
      in the sun) can cause minor fogging in the tail lights; Tesla
      considers light fogging normal since the housings are vented.
      Pre-refresh cars (4 discrete tail lights): if fogging shows up
      evenly across all 4, Tesla is unlikely to act on it — asymmetric
      fogging has a better chance of getting fixed. Juniper-era (2025+,
      single full-width light bar): the same "compare across 4 lights"
      logic doesn't apply — instead, heavy water droplets (not just light
      fogging) are the signal a seal has actually failed and warrants
      service.
- [ ] **Trim liner adhesion** — poor adhesive or application can let glued
      trim pieces (e.g. liftgate trim) start peeling after just a couple
      months. Keep an eye on glued parts and schedule service for anything
      coming loose.
- [ ] **No spare tire is included** — stock a tire patch/plug kit and a
      portable air compressor in the frunk or trunk so you have a plan
      before you need one (in addition to the Tesla app's roadside
      assistance).
- [ ] **Set your daily charge limit correctly for your battery chemistry**
      — check the Charging screen (in the app or car: Controls > Charging)
      for your battery type first; the car shows a recommended limit as
      you move the slider, and tapping the % number jumps to an exact
      value. Standard (NMC/nickel) batteries: charge to 80-90% daily, and let it
      hit 100% a few times a year (leave ideally within ~30 minutes,
      same day at most) so the BMS can recalibrate. LFP batteries (common on Standard Range trims): the
      opposite — Tesla recommends charging to 100% regularly, about once
      a week, for accurate BMS/range calibration. Don't assume the "charge
      to 100% once, early on" rule of thumb applies — it depends on which
      battery you have and is an ongoing habit, not a one-time step. On
      the low end, avoid draining below ~20% routinely — deep discharges
      add wear the same way sitting at 100% does. If leaving the car
      parked for an extended period (weeks or more), set the limit to
      ~50% before you go — batteries store best near the middle of
      their range, not topped up. For airport or long-term parking
      specifically: also turn off Sentry Mode and Cabin Overheat
      Protection before you leave (the two biggest phantom drain
      sources — expect ~1%/day without them running), and resist
      checking the Tesla app while away — each check wakes the car and
      adds drain.
- [ ] **Use home/Level 2 charging as your daily default** — DC fast charging
      (Supercharging) adds heat and current stress that accelerates long-term
      degradation when used routinely. Occasional road-trip Supercharging is
      fine and what the network is designed for; the habit to avoid is relying
      on a Supercharger as your primary daily charger.
- [ ] **Navigate to the Supercharger in the car's nav to get full charging
      speed** — the car only preconditions the battery for fast DC charging
      if it knows you're heading to a charger. Just driving there without
      routing means no preconditioning, and you'll charge significantly
      slower when you arrive. Use voice ("navigate to the nearest
      Supercharger") or tap the charger pin on the map and hit Navigate —
      either triggers preconditioning automatically.
- [ ] **Use the physical brake pedal occasionally** even with one-pedal
      driving — regen braking rarely lets the pads contact the rotors,
      which lets light surface rust build up (worse in humid/salty
      climates). A firm stop on an empty road every so often scrubs it
      off. Enabling Brake Disk Wiping in settings (periodic, imperceptible
      light braking) helps automatically but doesn't fully replace this.
- [ ] **Run a battery health test if you want a baseline** (Controls >
      Service > Battery Health) — reports a percentage like iPhone
      battery health. Takes up to 20 hours. Useful to run once early in
      ownership and again if you ever suspect unusual degradation.
- [ ] **Confirm your warranty terms** if unsure — Basic Vehicle warranty is
      4yr/50,000mi; Battery & Drive Unit warranty is 8yr, with a mileage
      cap (100,000–120,000mi) that varies by trim/configuration. Check
      your specific config rather than assuming — trim names have also
      changed (e.g. "Long Range" is now "Premium").
- [ ] **Don't leave Sentry Mode on at home** — it keeps the car awake
      overnight and can meaningfully shorten 12V battery life. Set up
      HomeLink or a Sentry excluded location for your garage/driveway
      instead.
- [ ] **Check tire pressure seasonally** — basic physics: ~1 PSI drops
      per 10°F of ambient temperature fall, so a 30° swing between seasons
      means ~3 PSI lost without touching anything. Tesla recommends 42 PSI
      for most Model Y configurations. Check in fall and spring at minimum.
      In-car: swipe right on the main screen or Controls > Service.
- [ ] **Watch tire tread and get rotations scheduled** — every ~6,250mi
      (10,000km), or sooner if tread depth differs by 2/32in (1.5mm) or
      more between tires. Model Y wears tires faster than a comparable
      gas car (weight + torque), so skipping this costs you a set of
      tires early.
- [ ] **Get brake fluid checked every 2 years**, regardless of mileage —
      it absorbs moisture over time even with the physical brake pedal
      barely used.
- [ ] **Get the cabin air filter replaced every 2 years** — there are
      **2 cabin filters** in the passenger footwell. If your car has
      Biodefense Mode (check Climate settings), there are also **4 HEPA
      filters located under the frunk** (separate from the cabin filters,
      replaced every 3 years). Telltale sign either set is overdue: musty
      smell when you first turn on the AC.
- [ ] **Use the in-car maintenance schedule** — Controls > Service (or
      Tesla app > Service) shows every upcoming maintenance item and when
      it's due based on your mileage and time. It tracks cabin filter,
      tire rotation, brake fluid, AC desiccant, and more automatically.
      Most owners don't know it exists; following it means nothing gets
      missed and nothing gets replaced too early.
- [ ] **Log completed maintenance back into the car** — after any filter
      replacement (DIY or shop), go to Controls > Service > Maintenance,
      select the item, and tap Record to confirm the date. This resets
      the countdown so the car tracks your actual replacement, not just
      the factory baseline.
- [ ] **Keep rubber jack pads in your trunk** and bring them to any
      non-Tesla shop. The four chassis lifting points are recessed holes
      in the frame rails, very close to the battery pack — a standard
      floor jack without a pin-seated pad can slip and contact the casing.
      Damage from improper lifting is not covered under warranty.
- [ ] **Use only a Tesla-certified collision repair shop after any
      accident** — non-certified shops can misalign cameras (breaking FSD
      calibration), reset airbag modules incorrectly, and make structural
      repairs that look fine cosmetically but aren't. Find a certified
      shop via the Tesla app > Service after any incident; wait times at
      certified shops can be long in smaller markets, so book early.
- [ ] **Turn off auto-fold mirrors before winter** (Controls > Mirrors >
      Auto-Fold) — if mirrors auto-fold overnight and temperatures drop
      below freezing, they can freeze in the folded position and fail to
      open when you start driving. Many owners leave this off year-round.
- [ ] **Know the app's Unlatch Door shortcut for frozen handles** — the
      flush door handles can freeze shut in winter. In the Tesla app,
      long-press any shortcut button to see all available actions; find
      "Unlatch Door" and add it to your shortcuts. Triggers the door
      electronically so you can get in without forcing the handle.
- [ ] **Clean interior surfaces with mild automotive cleaner or damp
      microfiber only** — no Windex, ammonia-based cleaners, bleach, or
      general household cleaners on the vegan leather seats, door panels,
      or steering wheel. Harsh chemicals strip the protective coating and
      cause cracking and discoloration within 1–2 years. The steering
      wheel is the worst offender (constant skin contact + wrong cleaner).
- [ ] **Avoid rotating-brush automatic car washes** — the spinning
      brushes can mar paint and trim; use a touchless wash or hand-wash
      with the two-bucket method (separate wash and rinse buckets)
      instead.
- [ ] **Enable Car Wash Mode before any automatic wash** (Controls >
      Service > Car Wash Mode) — locks the charge port closed, disables
      wipers, keeps mirrors from auto-folding, and turns off rain-sensing
      wiper activation. Without it, any of those can trigger mid-wash.
- [ ] **Reset camera calibration if FSD drifts consistently** (Controls >
      Service > Camera Calibration) — if FSD repeatedly rides too close
      to one side of the lane and software updates haven't fixed it, a
      calibration reset clears the stored baseline. You drive manually
      for a bit while it recalibrates, then FSD resumes. Normal wear and
      alignment shifts can throw calibration off over time.
- [ ] **Know the screen reboot shortcut** — if the touchscreen freezes,
      goes black, or becomes unresponsive, press and hold both scroll
      wheels on the steering wheel simultaneously for ~30 seconds. The
      screen goes black, the Tesla logo returns, and the system restarts.
      The car keeps driving normally during a reboot and autopilot stays
      engaged — it's just restarting the UI.
- [ ] **Install software updates promptly** rather than deferring them —
      see the Firmware/OTA glossary entry for why a delivery-day issue
      might resolve itself this way. Set an overnight auto-install window
      (Controls > Software) so updates don't interrupt your day.

## Winter Prep & Operation

- [ ] **Blue snowflake on the display = battery too cold** — the car
      limits power and regen until the pack warms up. It goes away on its
      own as you drive, or immediately with preconditioning before you
      leave. Not a fault — normal cold-weather behavior.
- [ ] **Don't let the battery drop below 10% in winter** — at extreme
      cold, a deeply depleted battery can be very difficult to recover.
      One owner reported 3 days plugged into a Level 1 (120V) outlet at
      -30°C before the car would accept charge — the charger lacked enough
      amperage to heat the battery. Level 2 is especially important in
      cold climates.
- [ ] **Winter tires are strongly recommended if you get real snow** —
      AWD helps you accelerate but does nothing to help you stop. Winter
      tires use a softer rubber compound that stays grippy below freezing.
      For RWD specifically, winter tires aren't optional — they're a
      safety necessity. Also check and top up tire pressure throughout
      winter; cold air drops PSI (see the tire pressure item above).
- [ ] **Use winter-grade washer fluid** — never water. Standard fluid
      freezes in the lines and can rupture them. Stock up before winter
      and keep a spare jug in the frunk; you'll go through it faster than
      expected when roads are salted. Bring a funnel — pouring directly
      from a jug is messy.
- [ ] **Apply a rust-inhibiting coating (e.g. Fluid Film) to the
      undercarriage before winter** — spray nuts, bolts, hinges, and
      panel seams where metal intersects. Road salt attacks exposed metal
      all winter; 5–10 minutes of treatment in the fall makes a
      meaningful difference over the years.
- [ ] **Treat rubber door seals with rubber conditioner before winter**
      — prevents doors freezing shut in overnight cold. A frozen door at
      6am is a bad start to any day, and forcing a frozen handle can
      damage the mechanism.
- [ ] **Put wipers in service position before an overnight storm** — this
      lifts the blades off the glass so they don't freeze to the
      windshield. To set: Controls > Service > Wiper Service Mode.
- [ ] **Leave mirrors unfolded before a freezing rain or ice storm** —
      if auto-fold is on and mirrors freeze in the folded position, the
      motors can strain or fail trying to open them. Either turn off
      auto-fold for winter (see the auto-fold item above) or manually
      unfold them before parking.
- [ ] **Defrost remotely via the Tesla app before you head out** — Climate
      > Defrost in the app runs the defroster and heated mirrors. Combine
      with a scheduled departure for a fully clear car before you step
      outside.
- [ ] **Use Chill mode on slick or icy roads** (Controls > Dynamics >
      Acceleration > Chill) — significantly tames the instant torque
      delivery, making the car much easier to manage on low-traction
      surfaces. Switch back to Standard when roads clear.
- [ ] **Know Slip Start for getting unstuck** (Controls > Dynamics) —
      temporarily disables traction control to let the wheels spin and
      break free from snow, ice, or mud. Use it only when stuck, then
      turn it back off once moving.

## Settings That Save Battery

- [ ] **Turn off Keep Accessory Power On** (Controls > Charging) unless
      you're camping or need the USB/12V ports while parked — it causes
      phantom drain (one owner went from ~4%/day to under 1%/day). The car
      sends a phone notification if you leave it on.
- [ ] **Set Cabin Overheat Protection to No A/C (fan only) or Off** if
      you park in shade or a garage — full A/C mode drains noticeably
      (~0.75 kWh reported) and runs up to 12 hours or until the battery
      hits 20%. Tradeoff: a hotter cabin; never rely on it for pets or
      kids.
- [ ] **Turn on the Charge Port Heater only when the port is iced**
      (Controls > Service > Charge Port Heater; 2024.44+ software on
      select vehicles). It also runs automatically when you precondition
      the cabin or navigate to a Supercharger.
- [ ] **Check if your utility offers a time-of-use or EV-specific rate
      plan** — many utilities have plans where overnight/off-peak rates
      are significantly cheaper than peak hours. Sign up before you
      start charging; some plans require enrollment to activate the
      lower rates.
- [ ] **Use Schedule for off-peak charging and cabin preconditioning** while
      plugged in — cheaper rates, and cabin preconditioning draws grid power
      instead of battery. Use "End by" (not "Start at") when setting
      the schedule: the car back-calculates when to begin charging so
      it finishes at your target time regardless of how depleted it is
      when you plug in. For a road trip where you need 100%: set a
      one-time schedule ending at your departure time and turn off the
      repeating weekly schedule — otherwise the car hits 100% at 6am
      and sits there for hours before you leave, which is exactly what
      you're trying to avoid.
- [ ] **Enable Low Power Mode on Auto** (Controls > Charging > Low Power
      Mode > Auto) — automatically disables battery-draining features
      like Sentry Mode when the battery drops below a threshold you set
      (10–20%). Prevents the car from draining itself further when
      already low.

## Public Charging Etiquette & Fees

- [ ] **Move your car promptly once charging finishes** at a Supercharger
      — don't treat a stall as a parking spot. A 5-minute grace period
      applies before fees kick in (see Idle Fee / Congestion Fee below).
- [ ] **Avoid charging past ~90% at a Supercharger** unless you actually
      need the range — charging slows dramatically past that point, tying
      up the stall for marginal gain (and can trigger a congestion fee,
      see below).
- [ ] **Know paired stalls can share power** — charging two cars on the
      same power-sharing pair simultaneously can throttle both to a
      slower rate; if an open unpaired stall is available, prefer it.
- [ ] **Spot slower V2 (150kW) stalls before plugging in** — a silver ring
      around the cable just below the connector marks a V2 stall (thicker,
      non-liquid-cooled cable); V3+ (250kW) cables are thinner, lighter,
      and have no ring. Tapping the Supercharger pin in nav also shows
      "Up to 250kW" for V3+ stalls. Saves you from plugging in, watching
      the rate, and moving stalls after the fact.
- [ ] **Don't unplug someone else's car**, even if it looks finished.
- [ ] **Don't camp a Destination Charger** if you're not actually staying
      there — they're typically free/slow and limited in number, meant
      for while-you're-there charging (hotel, business), not general
      parking.
- [ ] **Report broken stalls** — if a charger won't connect, the
      connector looks cracked, or anything else goes wrong, report it
      via the network's app or the phone number on the unit before you
      leave. Networks often have no way of knowing unless a driver tells
      them; reporting takes 60 seconds and fixes it for the next person.
- [ ] **Know Emergency Driveaway exists** (software 2026.38.3+, US
      Superchargers only) — if you need to leave urgently while still
      plugged in, shift into Drive and tap Activate on the Emergency
      Driveaway prompt; the car will pull free of the cable. The event
      is automatically recorded and sent to Tesla; misusing it can result
      in liability up to $25,000 for cable/equipment damage. It's a
      genuine last-resort safety feature, not a shortcut for forgetting
      to unplug.

## Retraining Gas-Car Habits

- [ ] **Treat the range number on the dash as a rough estimate, not gospel**
      — it's constantly recalculating based on recent driving, temperature,
      speed, and climate control use. It will shift noticeably season to
      season and trip to trip; budget more buffer than you would with a
      fuel gauge. Note: Tesla builds in a ~3 kWh reserve, so hitting 0%
      displayed doesn't mean the car stops — expect roughly 10–20 miles
      of actual buffer. Don't rely on it, but don't panic the moment the
      display hits zero either.
- [ ] **Avoid standing water deeper than ~10cm (4in), and never drive
      into flowing water** — the battery is sealed, but Model Y's HVAC
      pressure-release vents sit in the sub-trunk area and can let water
      into the cabin if the water outside is higher than the lower trunk.
      There's no "Wade Mode" (that's Cybertruck-only). If already in
      water, keep moving slowly and steadily rather than stopping — a
      wake pushes water away from the vents, stopping lets it seep in.
      If it stalls, don't try to restart it; have it towed and inspected
      before driving again (water intrusion can cause delayed electrical
      failures that don't show up immediately).
- [ ] **Plan for significant range loss in cold weather** — depending on
      the model and conditions, range can drop 20–30% in winter (heat pump
      models lose less). Cross-check real-world winter data for your
      specific trim before relying on the full rated range for a long trip.
- [ ] **Expect weaker regen braking on a cold start** — you'll see a
      "Regenerative braking limited" alert until the battery warms up,
      meaning you'll need the physical brake pedal more than usual right
      after starting cold. Battery preconditioning (set a scheduled
      departure time, or navigate to a Supercharger) shortens this window.
- [ ] **Don't expect the car to creep forward at a stop** — newer builds
      default to (and often only offer) Hold mode, which fully stops and
      holds the car when you lift off the accelerator, unlike a gas
      automatic's creep.
- [ ] **Skip the gas-car habit of idling to warm up** — precondition the
      cabin/battery via the app while still plugged in instead (uses grid
      power, not battery charge).
- [ ] **Use "Keep" in the app for short stops in hot or cold weather** —
      in the Tesla app's climate panel, tap Keep to hold the cabin at its
      current temperature while you're away. More efficient than shutting
      off and having to re-cool or re-heat from an extreme on return,
      especially for stops under 30 minutes.
- [ ] **Send navigation to the car from your phone before you leave** —
      in the Tesla app, tap the location/nav icon, search your destination,
      and send it to the car. The route (including any Supercharger stops)
      is ready the moment you get in. You can also set your departure
      state of charge and arrival target from the app, so the car
      recalculates stops before you've even started driving.
- [ ] **Don't skip route planning on longer trips** just because Trip
      Planner (nav) automatically adds Supercharger stops when needed —
      running low on charge is a bigger deal than running low on gas, and
      you can't just walk to a station with a can. Review the
      auto-planned stops (adjustable by dragging them on the touchscreen)
      rather than assuming they're optimal for your actual plans.
- [ ] **Swipe right on a waypoint to remove it mid-trip** — in the
      active route's waypoint list, swipe right on any stop to delete it
      on the fly. Works while driving.
- [ ] **Use smoother, more deliberate throttle input in low-traction
      conditions** — instant torque makes wheel spin easier to trigger
      than a gas car's more gradual power delivery, especially in rain or
      snow.
- [ ] **Don't rely on engine noise to alert pedestrians/cyclists** — the
      legally mandated low-speed exterior sound doesn't guarantee they'll
      hear you; drive extra cautiously in parking lots out of habit, not
      reflex.
- [ ] **Know it can't be flat-towed if disabled** — a flatbed or dolly is
      required, since there's no transmission neutral to disconnect the
      motors the way a gas car has.
